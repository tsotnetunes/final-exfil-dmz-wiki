# Activision Support: Call of Duty Security and Enforcement Policy
URL: https://support.activision.com/codpolicy
Captured: 2026-10-31 (page dated 31 Jul 2026)
Trust: official

Facts taken, in our words:
- The policy applies across Call of Duty games and lists the behaviour that leads to penalties; the page says the list is not complete.
- Prohibited behaviour includes: boosting (using exploits to gain XP, prestige, score, weapon levels or unlocks); getting around Activision's security; decompiling or reverse engineering the game; obtaining Call of Duty Points or content improperly; glitching (abusing an exploit, for example getting outside the map through its geometry); griefing (deliberately stopping others from playing as intended, such as killing your own team on purpose); false or spam reports; offensive behaviour; spoofing your identity or your hardware's identity; unsupported peripherals and apps used to cheat (input-modifying devices, modded controllers, IP flooders, lag switches); unauthorised software (aimbots, wallhacks, trainers, injectors, input-mapping tools), which may be closed automatically when detected.
- Penalties: warnings for some minor cases; temporary bans by severity; permanent bans, which can extend across past, current and future titles and are the only penalty that can be appealed; limited matchmaking with other restricted accounts, where no progression is earned; ranked restrictions; hardware bans on the device itself, which can cross titles and cannot be appealed; enforcement on the whole account across every linked device; for extreme or repeated cases, suspension of all accounts plus hardware bans.
- Extra penalties at the security team's discretion: resets of stats, emblems and weapon customisation; a full inventory reset or a ban from the inventory system; removal from or a block on leaderboards; revoking Points or items; rename, party, split-screen, chat or reporting restrictions.
- Player reports can support enforcement; making false reports is itself a violation.
