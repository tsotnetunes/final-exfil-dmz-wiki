# GameRiv: Call of Duty's new RICOCHET anti-cheat update targets DMA cheats
URL: https://gameriv.com/call-of-dutys-new-ricochet-anti-cheat-update-targets-dma-cheats/
Captured: 2026-10-31 (byline 24 Oct 2026; page metadata 23 Oct 2026)
Trust: third-party

Facts taken, in our words:
- Relays the official Call of Duty account's announcement of 23 Oct 2026: RICOCHET rolled out new detections aimed at DMA (direct memory access) cheats; they went live earlier that week, ahead of DMZ early access.
- DMA cheats use separate hardware outside the gaming PC to read information a player should not have, such as where enemies are, which helps them avoid software-based protection.
- Activision did not explain how the detections work, gave no count of banned accounts and said it will keep adjusting as cheat makers change their methods.
- Activision shared screenshots of players complaining about permanent and hardware bans; the article does not treat these as official policy.
- Mentions the Secure Attestation Wizard version 1.3.0 (21 Oct 2026), AMD 3.x.0.x firmware as the most common current problem and Lenovo users reporting more issues.
