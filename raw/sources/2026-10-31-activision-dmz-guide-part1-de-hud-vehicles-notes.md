Facts taken, in our words:
- HUD, numbered in the guide: Tac-Map (objectives, key places, danger zones, enemy bases, important buildings, ammo and armour caches, spreading radiation, your position and facing, nearby enemies, pings, POIs); Time Remaining (when the radiation storm intensifies, no minutes given); Objectives (your and your squad's operations and Side Ops on the left, DMZ Orders on the right and on the Tac-Map); Compass (heading 1 to 360 and the name of the POI you are in); Threat Level (below the compass at top centre, updates when you enter a new place, human Operators not counted); Hostiles Kill Count (enemies killed this deployment; when full, a hunter squad comes and you are Marked for Death); Squad HUD (portrait, squad colour, name, health, plates, current armour, Downed, Wounded or Dead, temporary health from painkillers); DMZ Cash carried by each Operator; equipment capacity (spare plates, gas mask, parachute); Backpack button with a sort key; weapon display (ammo, fire mode, rarity and attachments); secondary weapon; melee, Tactical and Lethal with counts; Field Upgrades and Fire Support.
- The Backpack menu lets you move items into your loadout or use consumables directly, such as bandages, extra plates and field upgrade crates.
- The Ping Wheel gives visual commands when voice is unavailable, compromised or unwise, including asking for a ceasefire or to join another squad.
- On the Tac-Map you can centre on yourself, move a cursor, zoom, set or clear pings, cancel the current operation and inspect terrain and hazards; DMZ Order objectives on its right side can be pinged for the squad.
- Stealth: silent melee, throwing knives and suppressed shots let you move through higher-threat areas unnoticed; threat drops when you hide in shadow, take cover, move to rural areas, move quietly and get out of enemy sight and earshot.
- Exfil: exfil points light up across Hajin with a column of green smoke; you call the twin-rotor helicopter, defend the area as pressure rises until it lands, and board to finish the deployment; alternatives are the Fulton system or a flare; flares are very loud and visible and can draw ambushes; other squads may ambush exfil points.
- Vehicles: Camper (unarmed, unarmoured); Sedan (small civilian car, not for combat); Grizzly (Soviet-based, light armour, fast); Dreadnought (Aston Martin four-wheel-drive tactical off-roader with advanced armour); Polaris Dagor A1 (fast deployment over extreme ground, carries a full squad and its gear); LTV (light combat platform, optional mounted .50 calibre gun); Cargo Truck (diesel platform, two-seat cab, flat bed); D50 Radar Flatbed (same platform with a vehicle-mounted drone); Train and Tram (not player-controlled); RHIB (fast special-operations boat); Heli (twin-engine attack helicopter); Heavy Chopper (armoured troop transport with decoys against missiles, hovers on autopilot while fuel lasts if the pilot leaves; not player-controlled); Monarch (twin-rotor heavy-lift helicopter for friendly infil and exfil; not player-controlled).
- DMZ vehicles must be repaired and refuelled, Warzone vehicles do not; Gas Stations repair and refuel and refill carried Gas Cans; Marine Gas Stations are small piers with two pumps for boats, used most by armoured patrol boats and RHIBs, and usable by well-placed land and air vehicles; some Traits help, for example getting out to repair a flat tyre.
- Infil: the base's Deploy station leads through a wire gate to a heavy military transport helicopter that takes you into each deployment.
