Yes. RICOCHET Anti-Cheat covers DMZ with three launch protections, Server Culling, DMA detection and stronger hardware enforcement, which Activision says have worked together in Multiplayer and DMZ since day one [[src-cod-x-ricochet-week-one-2026-10-30]] [clm_16b6ee610da3]. On PC it also relies on TPM 2.0 and Secure Boot, which every PC player must enable [[src-activision-pc-specs-2026-09-29]].

## What does each protection do?
| Protection | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Server Culling | a new way to fight cheating in MW4, used in DMZ from day one [clm_1f0839d8acfd] [clm_16b6ee610da3] |
| DMA detection | RICOCHET detections aimed at DMA cheats, which use extra hardware [clm_dee5007d4538] [clm_fed37aaa79dd] |
| Stronger hardware bans | make it harder for banned cheaters to come back on new accounts (reported) [clm_754b64a3d133] |
| TPM 2.0 and Secure Boot | required on PC and used by RICOCHET with server-side detection [[src-activision-pc-specs-2026-09-29]] |
| Account security | two-factor sign-in and email checks [[src-activision-pc-specs-2026-09-29]] |

The developers list the same measures for DMZ in their 9 Oct Q&A (as relayed) [[src-westie-dmz-ama-recap-2026-10-11]]. Players also note the protections have been active in DMZ since launch (reported) [clm_cd65cdd88e65].

## Is it working?
After the first week, on 30 Oct 2026, Activision said reports of suspected cheating were lower than at last year's launch, fewer of the main cheat sellers were active, and response times were holding; it gave no numbers [[src-cod-x-ricochet-week-one-2026-10-30]].
