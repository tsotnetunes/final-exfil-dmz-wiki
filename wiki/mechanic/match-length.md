A DMZ deployment lasts up to roughly three quarters of an hour. Before launch Infinity Ward said matches would run 40 to 50 minutes, up from about 30 in the 2022 beta, and that both numbers were still being tuned [[src-gamerfuzion-dmz-solo-play-2026-10-03]]. You can leave much earlier: a deployment ends for you when you extract or die.

## How does the timer work?

The Time Remaining clock on your HUD counts down to the moment the radiation storm gets worse [[src-activision-dmz-guide-part1-2026-10-05]]. One guide says it starts at about 30 minutes (reported) [[src-conquestcapped-dmz-guide-2026-10-26]]. When it reaches zero you have a little under 15 minutes before radiation covers the whole map [clm_a15e34798a66] [[src-conquestcapped-dmz-guide-2026-10-26]]. Exfils inside the radiation close (the one nearest the storm first, one guide says), and once the others are gone a final exfil stays open and shows in white [clm_c772bf67ab75] [[src-conquestcapped-dmz-guide-2026-10-26]]. More on [[radiation-storm]] and [[exfil]].

## Does everyone start at the same time?

Yes, for now. In their 9 Oct 2026 Q&A the developers said all squads drop into a match together, and that this is how it works "currently" [[src-gamerfuzion-dmz-60-players-2026-10-12]]. If you crash, you can rejoin while the match is still running [[src-westie-dmz-ama-recap-2026-10-11]]; see [[server-and-performance-issues]].

## What is not confirmed?

The official guides give no minutes for the Time Remaining clock or for how fast the radiation spreads, so the 30 and 15 minute figures come from players and guides. They may change with patches or with [[season-1]], which starts on 6 Nov 2026 [[src-insider-gaming-season-1-date-2026-10-14]].
